# Growth plan: 55 → 1,000 WAU by 2026-12-31

Researched 2026-10-03. Constraints from Noah: **~55 WAU today** (mostly testers
and friends, a few from Reddit and App Store search), **under $100/month**,
**about 1 hour/week** of his time. Everything else is done by agents.

## The math

1,000 WAU is about 18x in 13 weeks. Working backwards:

- Since GA, **44% of new users ever catch a plane** (PR #225 analysis). Among
  the users who catch, about 91% come back on a later day after catch 2–3.
- Rough steady state: `WAU ≈ installs this week + activated users still
  around from earlier weeks`. At 44% activation and a typical
  retention curve, WAU in late December ≈ **2.5x weekly installs**. That means
  about **400 installs/week** by December.
- If first-catch activation rises to about 65%, the multiplier becomes about
  3.5x and the target drops to about **280 installs/week**. **Activation is the
  cheapest lever on the board:** it lowers the bar for every channel below.

Honest odds: a plan that never goes viral probably lands at **300–500 WAU**.
Reaching 1,000 needs one breakout: an Apple feature, a TikTok post that takes
off, or a creator video. So the plan runs several cheap shots at a breakout
while the share and invite loops make sure each one compounds.

## Competitive reality

**Skycards by Flightradar24** is the same game: catch real planes overhead,
card collection, rarity, battles. It has 500k+ App Store downloads and is
visibly viral on TikTok
([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/skycards-by-flightradar24/id6737029127),
[FR24 blog](https://www.flightradar24.com/blog/flight-tracking-news/just-for-fun/skycards-collectible-aviation-card-game/)).
Zephy, Overflight and Overhead cover "what's that plane" lookup, and 9to5Mac
featured two of them this year.

Two consequences:

1. **Positioning:** "Every catch is a plane you actually saw" (STRATEGY's key
   message) is the wedge. Lead with point-at-the-sky AR and authenticity
   ("you have to really see it"), not with "collect planes".
2. **Skycards proves the demand.** Its audience is reachable through content
   that rides the same hashtags and hooks. Apple rejects competitor names in
   keywords (guideline 2.3.7), so compete through content, not metadata.

## Strategy: one content engine, two loops, three lottery tickets

### Foundation (product work, agent-built)

| # | Item | Why | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Ship first-catch onboarding** (revive PR #225 for 1.2.1/1.3) | Biggest WAU multiplier. 21 of 66 new users had a catchable plane and never tapped it | M |
| F2 | **9:16 story-format share card** for catches, carrying the invite/App Store link and a QR code | Most shares go to Stories, where links get dropped; the QR and URL survive a screenshot | S |
| F3 | **Server-rendered OG image for `/c/CODE`** (from `GET /v1/invites/:code/preview`) | WhatsApp/Slack invites currently preview as the generic App Store page | S |
| F4 | **"Tailspot Wrapped 2026"** card, live about Dec 1 (rarest catch, types, airlines, streak, percentile) | Spotify Wrapped drove +21% downloads in launch week; lands before the deadline | M |
| F5 | **Holiday set:** "Peak Season Freighters" (UPS/FedEx/DHL/Atlas 747/767/777F), Nov 15 – Dec 31 | Real holiday hook that doesn't break "the catch is real" (no fake Santa plane) | S |

### Loop 1: share and invite (product does the distribution)

Catch → story card → friend installs. Challenge → invite link → friend joins.
F2 and F3 fix the leaks; Challenges ships in 1.2.0. Watch
`catch_share_opened`, `challenge_invite_shared` and
`devices.referred_by_challenge_id` weekly. **Target: 0.15 invited installs per
active user per week.**

### Loop 2: automated content engine (the main AI lever)

A daily cloud agent (Claude Code routine on cron) that:

1. Pulls something notable from **public ADS-B** (adsb.lol) and **aggregate**
   catch stats: "A380 over Berkeley today", "rarest Tailspot catch this
   week", "a 747-8F over Bali".
2. Renders a 5–6 slide carousel: Noah's own catch photos, app screenshots,
   real data. **No fabricated people or "UGC".** TikTok labels and
   down-ranks undisclosed AI content.
3. Writes the hook and caption, then posts through Postiz (free/cheap tier):
   auto-publish to Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts; **TikTok as drafts**,
   since the API stays private-only until TikTok's audit.
4. Next morning, reads each post's view counts and rotates in the
   best-performing hook formats. One account, avgeek-native voice.

Noah's part: open TikTok drafts, add a trending sound, post (~1 min each). The
best evidence for indie apps (Oliver Henry's "Larry" agent: 500K TikTok views
in 5 days for about $0.50/post) came from exactly this shape. The realistic
floor is about 100 installs total, so treat it as volume plus one or two
outliers. **Kill rule:** if 30 posts average under 1k views, change formats
before adding volume.

Spin-off: the same renderer feeds a labeled Bluesky/Mastodon bot ("notable
aircraft over the Bay Area") for free. It brings few installs, but it builds a
content archive.

**Privacy guardrail** (privacy policy §5): user photos never leave the phone,
and catch GPS can't be shown publicly. The engine uses public ADS-B, aggregate
counts, Noah's photos, and later **opt-in** "submit to Tailspot's feed"
photos, which needs a policy update first.

### Always-on, low effort

- **ASO:** the agent drafts 3–5 custom product pages (plane identifier,
  planespotting, flight tracker, airport) with their own keywords; since
  July 2025 these rank in organic search. Monthly **In-App Events** (Rare Catch
  Week, Holiday Freighters, Wrapped) re-engage lapsed users from search.
- **Reddit:** never automated (the 2025–26 crackdown bans bot posting). The agent
  watches r/aviation, r/planespotting and r/flightradar24 for "what plane is
  that / is there an app" threads and drafts replies; Noah posts 2–3 a week
  from his account. One honest "I built this, here's v1.2 with challenges"
  post per subreddit, timed to a release.
- **Website:** add robots.txt, a sitemap and about 20 real-data pages (major
  airports, top types) only where there's genuine data. SEO takes 2–4 months
  to rank, so it mostly pays off in 2027. Not a web app, so it stays inside
  STRATEGY's "not doing".
- **Paid:** skip Search Ads. At about $2–4 per install, $100 buys 25–50 installs.
  Spend the budget on Postiz and image rendering.

## Automation stack

- **Cloud routines** (Claude Code on a schedule): the daily content agent, the
  Monday growth report, weekly outreach drafting, Reddit thread scouting.
- **Postiz** for posting; Gmail drafts for outreach (nothing auto-sends).
- **Read-only PostHog personal API key + App Store Connect API key** stored as
  environment secrets for the report agent. Never committed.

## Metrics (weekly report)

WAU, installs by source (Website / Catch Share / Challenge Invite campaigns +
untagged), activation (install → first catch), W1/W4 retention of activated
users, shares and invites per active user, content views and click-throughs.
Every tactic gets a two-week check; drop the ones that don't move installs.

## Not doing

Fake AI "UGC" people, Reddit automation, auto-sent email, competitor names in
App Store metadata, buying installs, user photos or locations in public content
without opt-in. All of them either break a ToS or break the "every catch is real"
brand, and either costs more than it earns.
