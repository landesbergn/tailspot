How much should the sky give away?

You said you want the game to be about seeing something and then catching it, not being told where it is. You liked Sky Watch for being exploratory, didn't want the server holding a location, and don't want sound. Five designers each took one angle on that, and a judge picked four concepts that span the range. They're ordered here from “tells you nothing” to “tells you where”, with Inbound as originally pitched at the end for comparison. Every frame is drawn on a real field shot from Berkeley, with the current HUD chrome redrawn on top.

GIVES AWAY NOTHING

1 · Spot to Name

The camera never labels a plane you haven't found. You look with your own eyes and tap the speck. If it's a real plane, the brackets close on it and its callsign types on. The name is the reward for spotting.

Keeps back

Everything. You choose where to look, find the speck yourself, and commit with a tap.

Privacy

Nothing new. It uses the same polls the open camera already makes.

Build

Mostly exists already. Tap-to-reveal already resolves a tap to a plane; this makes it the only way to get a label. Ship behind a flag and A/B it on first-catch rate.

Risk. This is the purest version of your sentence, and the hardest game. New users already struggle (21% see a plane and never catch it). Keep today's auto-labels until the first catch, then switch to spotting.

GIVES AWAY A STALE MOOD

2 · Sky widget (Sky Watch with no server and no push)

A lock-screen and Home Screen widget that reads the sky near you like weather: quiet, steady or busy, how many planes are above you, and the rarest tier up there. It never pushes and it's dated, so it reads as a forecast you check, not an alert.

Keeps back

Direction, distance, type and timing, and even whether that plane is still there by the time you're outside.

Privacy

The server keeps nothing. The widget asks for location when iOS refreshes it and makes the same anonymous request the open app makes. If you decline, it shows the app's last reading.

Build

A WidgetKit extension (a new target), sharing the reading through an App Group. iOS decides how often it refreshes, which is why every reading is dated.

GIVES AWAY IS IT WORTH LOOKING

3 · Sky Report (the empty-sky pill reads the conditions)

The grey “NO AIRCRAFT IN VIEW” pill becomes a conditions readout: how busy the sky is, how many planes are above your horizon, and the rarest tier up there. It answers “is it worth looking?” and never “where?”.

Keeps back

Which plane is the rare one, its bearing, height, distance, type and airline. You still scan the whole sky.

Privacy

Nothing new: it uses the open app's polls, and “new to you” is checked on the phone.

Build

Small. A pure function in the existing pill, held for 20 s so it doesn't flicker. It only counts planes you could actually turn and catch.

GIVES AWAY A CLASS AND A WINDOW

4 · Sighting report (an on-device Sky Watch push)

Opt-in and off by default. When the app goes to the background, it looks at the last sky it fetched. If something rare will pass within reach, it schedules one silent local notification that says what kind of plane, not which or where. It's capped at one a day.

Keeps back

Direction, height, distance, airline and exact type. You know a heavy is coming, but not from where.

Privacy

The server keeps nothing. The report is scheduled on the phone from the last tile the open app fetched, and the app reads no location in the background.

Limit

It only knows what was coming when you last closed the app, so it can't watch your sky all day. That's the cost of keeping the server out of it.

GIVES AWAY WHERE AND WHEN

For comparison · Inbound as pitched

This is the version from the level-up plan: chevrons on the edge of the frame point at planes about to come into view, with type, direction and time. It's the most effective at getting a first catch, and the furthest from “see it, then catch it”.

My read

A camera that rewards looking, and a widget for the heads-up

  1. Sky Report first. It's the smallest build, changes the screen people spend most of their time on, and gives nothing away but “is it worth looking?”. It needs no new data and no new permissions.
  2. Spot to Name as a flagged experiment for players with at least one catch. It turns your sentence into the core rule, and a tap on a speck you found is also a stronger “this catch is real” signal than an auto-label.
  3. The Sky widget is the Sky Watch to build: pull-only, dated, nothing on the server. Hold the Sighting report back unless the widget proves people want a push.
  4. Inbound as pitched is shelved. It answers “where?”, which is what you said the game shouldn't do.
  5. No sound kit. Haptics stay as they are, a single light tick at most.
